- **Almost no one gets named.** SOCi's index of 350,000+ locations found ChatGPT recommends about **1.2%** of them, Perplexity **7.4%** and Gemini **11%**, against **35.9%** in Google's local 3-pack.

A plumber, a dentist or a restaurant owner has a new question to ask: *"If someone types 'best dentist near me' into ChatGPT, am I in the answer?"* For most local businesses, today, the honest answer is no.

This post covers what the two largest 2026 studies found, why local AI results behave differently from Google Maps, and which steps are worth your time. It's written for owners of businesses with customers in a specific area: clinics, trades, restaurants, salons, law firms, gyms and shops.

**How to read the numbers.** Both studies come from companies that sell local marketing software. We link every figure to its source and flag where that matters.

## Are customers really asking AI for local businesses?

Yes, and the shift happened within a single year. BrightLocal's *Local Consumer Review Survey*, published in February 2026, asked 1,002 US adults where they get local business recommendations ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)):

| Source of local recommendations | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| AI tools | 6% | **45%** |
| Google | 83% | 71% |
| Facebook | 40% | 49% |

Two details matter for planning. Adults aged 30-44 lead, with 64% saying they've asked AI for a business recommendation in the past year, while only 24% of those over 60 have. And people still double-check: BrightLocal's follow-up on AI users found most cross-check a recommendation before contacting a business.

**What this means for you.** AI is now a real discovery channel for local services, but it sits beside Google and Facebook rather than replacing them. A customer may find you in ChatGPT, then verify you on Google reviews.

## How many local businesses does AI actually recommend?

Very few. SOCi's *2026 Local Visibility Index* tracked more than 350,000 locations across 2,751 multi-location brands and measured how often each channel recommended them ([SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/?p=37576), reported by [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/ai-local-visibility-report-2026-468085)):

| Channel | Share of locations recommended |
|---|---|
| Google local 3-pack | **35.9%** |
| Gemini | 11.0% |
| Perplexity | 7.4% |
| ChatGPT | **1.2%** |

SOCi describes ChatGPT as nearly 30 times more selective than the 3-pack ([SOCi](https://soci.ai/news/in-ai-driven-discovery-few-brands-are-chosen-most-disappear/)). It also found business details such as hours and addresses were only about 68% accurate on ChatGPT and Perplexity.

**Read with care.** The sample is multi-location brands, not single-shop owners, and SOCi is a vendor with no independent replication yet. The direction is clear anyway: **a spot in the AI answer is scarce, which is exactly why it's worth something.**

## Why do AI results change every time you ask?

This is the part owners find most frustrating, and BrightLocal's September 2026 *Local AI Visibility Study* quantifies it. It analysed about 200,000 searches across roughly 1,300 business locations on ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Google AI Overviews, with around 1.9 million citations ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/)):

- **Results rarely repeat.** Repeat searches shared only 21-33% of the same businesses.
- **Appearing once isn't staying.** A business that showed up came back in about half of later runs (50% on ChatGPT and AI Mode, 58% on AI Overviews).
- **Most businesses are occasional.** 71-80% appeared in half of the attempts or fewer.
- **Platforms disagree.** ChatGPT overlapped just 19-20% with either Google surface.
- **Google Maps is steadier.** Maps showed 99% agreement across attempts, against 91-96% for the AI platforms.
- **Answers are short.** ChatGPT named about 4.1 businesses per response, AI Mode 3.5 and AI Overviews 2.5.

**What this means for you.** Checking once and seeing you're missing, or present, tells you little. Judge visibility across many prompts over weeks, and expect Google Maps to stay the more reliable channel for now.

## Where does AI get its local recommendations from?

BrightLocal analysed the sources behind those 1.9 million citations ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/)):

| Source type | Share of citations |
|---|---|
| Business websites | **42%** (across 93% of the ~116,000 domains cited) |
| Google Business Profile | **28.5%** |
| Yelp | 9.5% |
| Facebook and other platforms | the remainder, frequently cited |

Two takeaways. First, **your own website is the single biggest source**, so a thin site with no service or location detail hurts you. Second, Google Business Profile is cited heavily, which fits the Google-based surfaces in the study.

There's one real disagreement. Whitespark's research on Bing Places found Facebook, not Yelp, was the most common review source across categories, and its author said Google Business Profile results weren't appearing in ChatGPT at the time ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/blog/want-to-rank-in-chatgpt-focus-on-these-review-sites-new-research/)). OpenAI doesn't publish where ChatGPT's local data comes from, so claims about a single "source of truth" are guesses. The safe reading is to keep **all** the major listings accurate.

## Does being close to the customer matter?

Yes, a lot, especially on Google's AI surfaces. In BrightLocal's data ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/)):

- **53-59%** of recommendations were within 5 km of the searcher, and **72-78%** within 10 km.
- Searching from different points in the same town gave only 36-47% overlap, so **your "ranking" depends on where the customer stands.**
- Only 3.5% of businesses on ChatGPT, and 25% on AI Overviews, appeared from every point.
- **Storefronts were mentioned more often than service-area businesses:** a 33.6% mention rate against 29.4%.
- ChatGPT reached much further out than Google: the radius covering 90% of its results was 287 km, against about 38-64 km for Google's AI surfaces.

**What this means for you.** If you're a plumber or electrician without a shopfront, don't hide your service area. Spell out each suburb or town you serve in plain text on your site, because that's how a chatbot knows you belong in the answer.

## Do reviews help you get recommended by AI?

Probably, but the evidence is correlation only. Insites reported that businesses recommended by ChatGPT and Perplexity averaged about 133 Google reviews against 11 for those the AI ignored ([Insites](https://insites.com/resources/five-insites/what-it-takes-to-rank-in-ai/)). That's a vendor study, and it can't separate "AI likes reviews" from "established businesses have both reviews and AI mentions".

Still, reviews are cheap to improve and useful in every channel. 71% of consumers in BrightLocal's survey still use Google for local recommendations, and BrightLocal reports 88% of AI users fact-check the reviews AI cites. A customer who gets your name from ChatGPT will look at your reviews next.

## What should a local business do about AI SEO?

No study has isolated what causes a recommendation, so we've ordered these by evidence and low cost. Everything below is a sensible use of time even if AI changes tomorrow.

1. **Fix your Google Business Profile.** It's 28.5% of citations and the base of Maps, the steadiest channel. Complete every field, choose accurate categories, list real services and keep hours current. Add photos and post updates.
2. **Make your name, address and phone number identical everywhere.** Check Google, Yelp, Facebook, Bing Places and your main industry directory. SOCi found AI tools get basic details wrong about a third of the time, and inconsistent listings make that worse.
3. **Write plain-text pages for every service and place.** One page per service, and one per suburb or town you serve, each stating who it's for, what it costs or how pricing works, and how to book. Business websites are 42% of citations, so give them something to cite.
4. **Answer real customer questions on your site.** Use the questions customers ask on the phone: *"How much does a boiler repair cost?"*, *"Do you take walk-ins?"*, *"Are you open Sundays?"* Put the direct answer in the first sentence. Webflow's tests of this format are in our [case study](/blog/webflow-geo-case-study).
5. **Ask for reviews steadily, on more than one platform.** Request a Google review after each job, and encourage Facebook and Yelp where your customers use them. Reply to every review, good or bad.
6. **Let AI crawlers in.** Check your robots.txt, your hosting security and your CDN aren't blocking search crawlers such as OAI-SearchBot, and that key text isn't hidden behind scripts. Our [guide to getting cited in ChatGPT](/blog/get-cited-in-chatgpt-ai-search) covers the three places this goes wrong.
7. **Track by asking.** Add "ChatGPT or another AI assistant" to your "how did you hear about us?" question, on the booking form and at the counter. Then test 10 to 20 realistic prompts each month and record whether you're named. Remember the results shift run to run, so look at the trend.

## What should local businesses not spend money on?

Be careful with these:

- **"Guaranteed ChatGPT ranking" packages.** The formula isn't known, the results shift between runs, and you can't simply buy a place in the answer.
- **Fake or bought reviews.** They break platform rules and AI tools lean on the same review sources.
- **An llms.txt file as your main strategy.** Webflow tested it and saw no significant lift, as covered in our [Webflow case study](/blog/webflow-geo-case-study).
- **Dropping Google.** Maps still reaches more businesses and is far more consistent than any AI channel in the data above.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get my local business recommended by ChatGPT?

Yes, but there's no guarantee. SOCi found ChatGPT recommended about 1.2% of the 350,000+ locations it tracked, so it's selective. The best-supported steps are an accurate Google Business Profile, consistent listings across directories, detailed service and location pages on your website, and steady reviews.

### How many people use AI to find local businesses?

BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults found 45% use AI tools to find local business recommendations, up from 6% in 2025. Google still led at 71%, and most AI users double-check a recommendation before contacting a business.

### Is Google Business Profile important for AI search?

It looks important. BrightLocal found Google Business Profile made up about 28.5% of citations across ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overviews. Whitespark reported not seeing GBP results in ChatGPT at the time of its research, and OpenAI doesn't publish its data sources, so keep Bing Places, Yelp and Facebook accurate too.

### Why does ChatGPT show a different business every time I ask?

Local AI answers are unstable. BrightLocal found repeat searches shared only 21-33% of the same businesses, and a business that appeared once came back in about half of later runs. Google Maps, by contrast, showed 99% agreement across attempts.

### Do service-area businesses have a harder time in AI search?

Slightly. BrightLocal found storefronts were mentioned 33.6% of the time against 29.4% for service-area businesses. List every area you serve in plain text on your site, and keep your Google Business Profile service area up to date.

### Do I need an agency for local AI SEO?

Not to start. The first five steps above are things an owner or office manager can do in a few weeks. Bring in help when you have many locations, need reviews and listings managed at scale, or want prompt tracking set up.

### How long does local AI SEO take to work?

There's no published timeline for local businesses. Listing and website fixes can be picked up as pages are re-crawled, but AI results vary from run to run, so judge progress over a few months of monthly testing rather than a single search.
